Product Background: Fuel 2.0 by York IE

Fuel 2.0 is York IE’s operating platform for startup founders, executives, and operators. It helps companies understand how their business is progressing across core value creation areas, with York IE services, product data, AI summaries, and operating signals brought into one view.

The product should feel like a serious startup operating dashboard, not a marketing site. It should match the Fuel visual style: dark navy background, compact cards, thin borders, calm data presentation, subtle green accents, and a premium enterprise SaaS feel.

Primary Purpose

Fuel 2.0 helps a startup answer:

- What progress are we making?
- What is York IE helping us with?
- What changed recently?
- What needs attention?
- Are we moving closer to stronger product, GTM, revenue, finance, and fundraising readiness?

Main Areas in “Your Journey”

The Your Journey section shows progress across York IE value creation tracks. Each track should show current health, milestones, updates, and what the company has achieved with York IE.

Core tracks:

1. Development
2. Marketing
3. RevOps
4. FinOps

There may also be supporting views for benchmarking, success comparison, fundraising readiness, or maturity stage, but the primary product should focus on progress across these four operating areas.

Development Track

The Development track shows how product and engineering work is progressing.

It should answer:
- What is currently being built?
- What roadmap items are active, completed, or planned for later?
- Are designs approved?
- Is development ready to start?
- Is code quality healthy?
- Are releases on track?
- What changed recently?

What to display:
- Project overview
- Active roadmap
- Sprint/release timeline
- Design status
- Design approval status
- Code quality summary
- Weekly quality trends
- Release notes
- Resource allocation
- Recent project updates
- Client-friendly AI summary

York IE / product sources:
- Hub: client info, service info, project updates, resource allocation, quotation/service context, meeting notes, internal notes, project status
- Jira: roadmap items, sprint status, issue counts, active/closed/future work, release notes
- Launchpad: design previews, design URLs, design status, approval status
- Pulse: code quality, stability, speed, risk, test/build health, deployment readiness, weekly quality summaries

Important logic:
Development can only start when the related Launchpad design is approved. In the prototype, assume design is approved.

Marketing Track

The Marketing track shows growth and go-to-market performance.

It should answer:
- Are marketing signals improving?
- Which channels are working?
- Where is demand coming from?
- What should the team focus on next?
- How is York IE helping create demand momentum?

What to display:
- Marketing snapshot summary
- Traffic trends
- Channel mix
- Organic search visibility
- Paid campaign performance
- Social performance
- Conversion funnel
- Key opportunities
- Watch items
- Link to full GTM dashboard
- Client-friendly AI summary

York IE / product sources:
- GTM Dashboard: overall marketing performance, campaign reporting, channel summaries
- Google Analytics: sessions, users, engagement, funnel data
- Google Ads: spend, conversions, CPA, campaign performance
- Meta: Facebook and Instagram paid/social performance
- LinkedIn: organic and paid engagement
- Semrush: keyword movement, backlinks, authority, SEO opportunities
- Search Console: organic clicks, impressions, search position, query opportunities

RevOps Track

The RevOps track shows revenue operations, pipeline, and sales process health.

It should answer:
- Is the pipeline organized?
- Are revenue motions clear?
- Are leads and opportunities moving through the funnel?
- Is CRM data healthy?
- Are there bottlenecks in the sales process?

What to display:
- Pipeline health
- CRM hygiene
- Funnel movement
- Lead-to-opportunity conversion
- Opportunity stages
- Forecast signals
- Revenue process gaps
- CAC or acquisition efficiency where available
- Recommended operating improvements
- Client-friendly AI summary

York IE / product sources:
- Hub: service engagement, RevOps project updates, meeting notes, resource allocation
- CRM integrations such as HubSpot or Salesforce: contacts, companies, deals, pipeline stages, activities, owner assignments
- GTM/marketing sources where attribution is needed
- York IE internal notes/playbooks for RevOps recommendations

FinOps Track

The FinOps track shows financial operations, runway, planning, and business readiness.

It should answer:
- How is the company tracking financially?
- What is the runway picture?
- Are budgets and forecasts clear?
- Is the company prepared for hiring, fundraising, or board-level decisions?
- What financial signals need attention?

What to display:
- Runway summary
- Burn rate
- Revenue movement
- Budget vs actuals
- Forecast summary
- Cash position
- Finance milestones
- Board/reporting readiness
- Fundraising-readiness signals
- Client-friendly AI summary

York IE / product sources:
- Hub: service context, finance project updates, meetings, internal notes, resource allocation
- Accounting/finance tools such as QuickBooks
- Payment/revenue tools such as Stripe
- Forecasting or board reporting data, if available
- York IE internal FinOps notes and recommendations

Connectors Store Concept

Fuel 2.0 may include a connector store or integration marketplace in the future.

The purpose of the connector store is to let companies connect tools that power the Journey views.

Examples:
- Development: Jira, Linear, Launchpad, Pulse
- Marketing: Google Analytics, Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn, Semrush, Search Console
- RevOps: HubSpot, Salesforce
- FinOps: QuickBooks, Stripe

This should feel like a Fuel-native setup experience, not a consumer app marketplace. It should be compact, dark, structured, and aligned with the rest of the product.

Design Direction

The design should match the Fuel screenshots:
- Dark navy/blue-black background
- Dark blue-gray cards
- Thin borders
- Compact dashboard spacing
- Small green accents only for active/positive/primary actions
- Calm executive-friendly UI
- Dense but readable data
- Minimal decoration
- No bright green backgrounds
- No overly playful illustrations
- No large marketing-style hero sections

Tone of Voice

Use client-facing language:
- “your roadmap”
- “your design approval”
- “your marketing snapshot”
- “your pipeline”
- “your runway”
- “your latest updates”

Avoid third-person phrasing like:
- “the client”
- “Patriot Pay has achieved”
- “client-safe”

Use clear, executive summaries:
“Your roadmap is active, design is approved, and quality signals are improving. Two edge cases are still being watched before release.”

Overall Product Goal

Fuel 2.0 should help a startup clearly see how York IE is helping them build a stronger business across product, marketing, revenue operations, and financial operations.

The experience should feel like a startup operating command center powered by York IE products, services, integrations, and AI summaries.
